Journal · Behind the piece

Illuminated: a court scene, signed with a single white stroke

Illuminated began with a painting that was made to be held. Mughal miniature painting grew in the court workshops of India from the sixteenth century. The pictures were small, made for albums and manuscripts, and meant to be studied from inches away: faces, textiles, tiles and architecture drawn in fine detail.

The image

The scene on this tee is a court gathering: scholars, musicians and a ruler under a canopy. There is a lot going on in it, and that is the point. Every few centimetres there is another small story.

Printing a small painting at full size

A miniature is meant to be seen close up, so scaling it up is a risk. We print it as an A3 back panel on 280gsm cotton, large enough to read from across a room and detailed enough to reward a closer look. The palette is saffron, vermilion, lapis and white.

The signature

Across the scene we laid the ERATO name in white Farsi calligraphy, as if the painting were being signed. It is one confident stroke over a very busy picture, and it gives the tee its character.

Why Washed Charcoal

The dark, softly faded stone-washed cotton lets the colour of the miniature glow. On a light shirt the painting would sit on the cloth. On charcoal it looks lit from behind.

How to wear it

Wear it oversized, with plain dark trousers and let the back do the work. If you prefer a lighter base, Caparison in Bone carries the same quiet confidence with a very different picture: a horse in a carpet.
