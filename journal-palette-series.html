Journal · The palette series

Four artworks, turned into their colour charts

The palette series started with a simple question: what if you could wear the colours of a picture without wearing the picture itself? Each tee in the series takes one famous image, pulls its colours out as a grid of forty-two squares, and sets the original image small in the middle, like a window.

Last Call (ED-01)

The source is Edward Hopper's Nighthawks, the 1942 painting of a late-night diner. Its colours are bottle green, olive, rust and brass. We printed it on a Bone tee so the dark of the scene comes through. See Last Call.

Jade Room (ED-02)

The source is Alfred Hitchcock's 1958 film Vertigo, and the glow of its green hotel room. Emerald, teal, mint and one flash of signal red, on Washed Charcoal, so it reads like a slide held up to a lamp. See Jade Room.

Kanagawa (ED-09)

The source is Hokusai's woodblock print The Great Wave off Kanagawa, from around 1831. Indigo, slate blue and the cream of old paper, on Washed Charcoal, so the sea sits on a dark sky. See Kanagawa.

Concierge (ED-11)

The source is Wes Anderson's The Grand Budapest Hotel and its pink-and-red hotel. Plum, vermilion, mustard and aubergine, on Washed Charcoal. See Concierge.

Why a colour chart

A colour chart is a quiet way to quote an image. People who know the source spot it at once. People who do not see a handsome grid of colour, and some ask. Either way the tee works on its own, and the conversation about where it comes from is a bonus.

All four are 280gsm stone-washed oversized tees with an A3 back print, USD 44 each.
