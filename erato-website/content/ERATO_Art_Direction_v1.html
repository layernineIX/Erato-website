A design studio that dresses the history of art. Bright, witty, a little impossible — composed with the restraint of a gallery.

01

The World

Concept

Not clothes with art printed on them. Art history, staged as fashion.

ERATO lives in a sunlit, slightly surreal world where the canon of art history is pulled into the present and gently misbehaves. A marble bust wears the tee. A Renaissance hand holds a modern object. Scale is wrong on purpose. The scene is beautiful, composed, and quietly funny — the wit comes from the idea, never from clutter.

Think Jacquemus's confidence and warmth, but the surrealism is sourced from museums, cinema and photography instead of the French countryside. Playful in concept, disciplined in execution. That single tension is the whole brand.

Always

  • One strong idea per image — an art-history reference, reframed
  • Bright, natural, directional light; warm and inviting
  • Generous empty space around the subject
  • Wit through concept and staging, not jokes or memes
  • Composed like a photograph, framed like a gallery

Never

  • Literal paintings slapped onto a shirt mockup
  • Busy grids, stock models, generic e-commerce energy
  • Dark, cluttered or over-decorated compositions
  • Cheap humour, distorted logos, meme captions
  • All four colours shouting at once — restraint always
02

Palette

Colour

A curated four-colour art palette — cool blue against warm gold, with a soft lime and a deep olive to ground them. Used like a painter, not a poster: mostly quiet neutrals, with these colours arriving one or two at a time. Blue is the primary anchor and lives in links, marks and one hero moment. Gold is the warm highlight. Lime tints soft section blocks. Olive grounds and sits as text on lime. The ERATO logo still flips black-on-light / light-on-dark over any of them.

The two neutrals are my recommendation to make four saturated colours feel chic rather than loud — a warm off-white and near-black. Easy to tune at review; your four colours are locked.

03

Typography

Type

One typeface, used the way a gallery uses a single material. Jost is a geometric sans in the Futura lineage — thin, precise, perfectly circular O, minimal — which sits right next to the ERATO wordmark. No serif, no second font. Chic comes from scale and space: headlines set huge and thin in uppercase with open tracking; body kept light and quiet. The type itself becomes a piece of art.

05

Layout & Motion

Feel

The site should feel like turning the pages of an art magazine that happens to move. Full-bleed imagery, oversized thin type, deliberate empty space, and slow, expensive motion — nothing bounces or rushes.

i

One idea per screen

Each scroll section holds a single image or statement. Space is the luxury.

ii

Image leads, text follows

The photograph arrives first and fills the frame; type is a caption to the art.

iii

Slow reveal

Elements fade and rise on scroll; images shift subtly. Cinematic pacing, never flashy.

iv

Edge-to-edge

Hero and campaign imagery bleed to the browser edges. No boxed, centred, template feel.

v

Editorial grid

Asymmetry over symmetry. Off-centre placement, mixed scale, magazine tension.

vi

Quiet interface

Nav and buttons whisper. Thin lines, small caps, blue used only where it matters.

06

Voice

Words

Intelligent, warm, a little dry. ERATO writes like a curator with a sense of humour — it knows the art history, wears it lightly, and never over-explains. Short lines. Confident. Occasionally winking.

07

In One Line

North Star

ERATO stages art history as sunlit, surreal fashion — playful in idea, gallery-disciplined in craft.

Everything we build — every image, page, headline and animation — is measured against that sentence. If it isn't warm, art-referential, composed and a little surreal, it isn't ERATO.
